# Survivor-Like Design Pattern Reference

A concrete, cited breakdown of how **Vampire Survivors**, **Brotato**, and **Halls of Torment** structure their core gameplay and progression loops. Built for a game-dev agent prototyping a Pokemon-themed survivor-like. Every substantive claim is sourced. Numbers are quoted where the source documents them; where a source gives a formula, the formula is reproduced so it can be ported directly.

> Genre framing: the survivor-like (also "bullet heaven," "reverse bullet hell," "autoshooter," "horde survivor") is a shoot-'em-up subgenre where the player steers one character through waves of enemies while attacks mostly fire automatically, unlocking periodic upgrades, with each run structured like a roguelite [Vampire Survivors–like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors%E2%80%93like){evidence:"a subgenre of shoot 'em up video games in which the player combats successive waves of enemies by maneuvering their character ... each successive run proceeding in the fashion of a roguelike"}. The flip from "hell" to "heaven" is a flip in the balance of power: you are the demi-god spitting out projectiles rather than the fragile dodger [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"in a bullet heaven, you are a demi-god spitting out millions on hordes of enemies"}.

## Vampire Survivors (poncle, 2022)

The genre-defining title, built around a single verb (move) with everything else automated [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"builds everything around a single verb: move. There's no manual aim, no attack timing, no complex inputs"}.

### Core loop & run structure
- A run is a single stage, targeting roughly 30 minutes of survival. At the 30-minute mark the game ends by spawning Death (the Reaper), who normally one-shots the player, so "winning" a run means surviving to that wall (and, optionally, killing the Reaper with a specialized build) [Survive 31 minutes, kill Death](https://www.polygon.com/23067263/inlaid-library-red-death-unlock-31-minutes-boss/){evidence:"once you reach the thirty-minute mark, the game ends with Death swooping in and killing you instantly. However, there is a way you can survive this encounter, and even defeat the grim reaper"}.
- Three nested loops: a micro loop (dodge while auto-attacking), a meso loop (survive 30 minutes and level up weapons), and a macro loop (unlock characters and stages between runs) [Understanding a Gameplay Loop](https://gamedesigning.org/learn/game-loop/){evidence:"a micro loop of 'dodge enemies while auto-attacking,' a meso loop of 'survive 30 minutes and level up weapons,' and a macro loop of 'unlock new characters and stages between runs'"}.
- Within a run you collect XP gems to level up, "floor chicken" to restore health, and other items; each level grants a choice among three or four weapons/passives [Vampire Survivors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors){evidence:"Each level increase gives the choice of three or four weapons and power-ups named 'passives'"}.
- **Meta-progression carries via Gold**: gold collected in-run is spent in the main menu on permanent PowerUps that apply to every character. This also eliminates "zero-progress" runs, so even a short run advances the baseline [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"spend it on PowerUp in the main menu to increase your starting stats ... eliminating 'zero progress' runs"}. Cumulatively the PowerUps roughly 2.5x damage and at least 2x health, a large hidden power swing the player barely notices [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"All cumulated powerups make a massive difference in raw stats: about 2.5x the damages and at least x2 health"}.
- Stages, characters, weapons, bestiary entries unlock through a large achievement list that gives constant side goals over repeated play [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"its immense achievements list, giving you constant side objectives while playing the same content repeatedly"}.

### Weapon/ability acquisition & evolution
- Weapons auto-attack; each behaves differently (Whip fires in the facing direction to clear groups, King Bible orbits for passive defense), and the player chooses which to upgrade [How Vampire Survivors built its own unlikely genre](https://store.epicgames.com/news/vampire-survivors-built-genre-autoshooter-bullet-heaven?lang=en-US){evidence:"the Whip attacks every few seconds in whatever direction your character is facing ... while the King Bible slowly spins around you, killing enemies at a distance and granting some passive defense"}.
- Inventory cap: 6 weapons and 6 passives, so specialization is forced; weapons have about 8 upgrade tiers and passives about 5, which converts "a reward every ~2.5 minutes" into "a reward about every 23 seconds" for smoother pacing [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"8 for weapons, 5 for passives ... rather than a reward every 2 min 30 ... you can have one every 23 seconds"}.
- **Evolution** is the signature system: a fully leveled base weapon plus a specified passive item (the "catalyst") transforms into a stronger evolved weapon when the player opens a qualifying treasure chest [Evolution](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Evolution){evidence:"The player must have a fully leveled base weapon and its counterpart ... The player must then collect a Treasure Chest that contains an evolution type reward"}.
- **Gating** is layered and deliberate:
  - For classic base-game recipes the passive only needs to be owned (level 1 is enough; the game only checks ownership) [Vampire Survivors Weapon Evolution Guide](https://rappy-world.com/guide/vampire_survivors_weapon_evolution_guide_best_combos_to_unlock){evidence:"For the classic base game recipes, level 1 is enough - the game only checks that you own it"}.
  - Post-1.0 and DLC recipes additionally require the passive to be fully maxed [Evolution](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Evolution){evidence:"most of the weapons after Version 1.0 and from the DLCs, all related passive items must also be fully leveled"}.
  - The chest itself is the second gate: evolution chests normally drop from bosses spawning at or after the 10-minute mark, with per-stage exceptions, so evolutions are time-gated as well as inventory-gated [Evolution](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Evolution){evidence:"this kind of chest is dropped by bosses that spawn at or after the 10-minute mark with some exceptions"}.
- **Variant transforms** worth copying: a *Union* merges two or more weapons into one slot (freeing inventory space), a *Gift* adds a bonus weapon/passive without replacing anything, and a *Morph* permanently transforms a specific character at a level threshold plus a relic catalyst [Evolution](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Evolution){evidence:"A Union is a combination of two or more weapons ... A Gift is an additional weapon or passive item ... A Morph involves a permanent transformation for a specific character"}. Canonical base-game pairs include Whip + Hollow Heart = Bloody Tear, Magic Wand + Empty Tome = Holy Wand, Knife + Bracer = Thousand Edge, King Bible + Spellbinder = Unholy Vespers, Garlic + Pummarola = Soul Eater [Evolution](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Evolution){evidence:"Whip + Hollow Heart = Bloody Tear ... Knife + Bracer = Thousand Edge ... Garlic + Pummarola = Soul Eater"}.

### Economy & shop
- Only one currency matters across the economy: in-run Gold is banked and spent on permanent PowerUps between runs (there is no in-run shop in the base loop). PowerUps apply to all characters and can be refunded freely to retest builds [PowerUps](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/PowerUps){evidence:"PowerUps are purchasable permanent improvements to stats that apply to all characters. They can be bought with Gold Coins"}.
- **PowerUp pricing scales super-linearly**: each rank raises the base cost of the next rank of the same PowerUp by the initial price (an InitialPrice of 200 makes 5 ranks cost 200+400+600+800+1000 = 3000 before fees), and a global "fees" term grows with the total number of ranks bought across all PowerUps [PowerUps](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/PowerUps){evidence:"the base cost of 5 ranks will be 200+400+600+800+1000=3000 (not accounting for fees)"}. Buying everything totals about 42.97M gold, of which ~39.7M is fees, so the late meta-grind is dominated by the escalating fee term [PowerUps](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/PowerUps){evidence:"The total cost of every PowerUp rank is 42,965,380, with ... 39,745,740 being from the fees"}.
- The chest is the in-run "slot machine": payouts range from 1 to 5 items and 100 to 1000 gold, with the first six chests hardcoded to 1-1-3-1-5 items to set expectations high before the real (low) odds kick in [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"the first six chests you get are hardcoded with respectively 1-1-3-1-5 items inside. This sets your expectations high before drastically reducing the odds"}.

### Enemy design & wave pacing
- Density escalates visibly within minutes: a few slow bats become a dense swirling mass of hundreds, with audio-visual feedback compounding alongside to signal growing power [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"What starts as dodging a few slow bats becomes navigating a dense, swirling mass of hundreds of enemies"}.
- Timed mini-bosses and escalating enemy types punctuate the run; the hard 30-minute Reaper is the terminal pressure spike [Survive 31 minutes, kill Death](https://www.polygon.com/23067263/inlaid-library-red-death-unlock-31-minutes-boss/){evidence:"once you reach the thirty-minute mark, the game ends with Death swooping in"}.
- Known weakness to design around: bosses tend to test the build's DPS rather than player skill, so a failed boss after a long run feels like failing an audit [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"When a boss tests your build instead of your skill, victory feels more like passing an audit than winning a fight"}.
- Pacing weakness: once mastered, the first few minutes feel slow and repetitive, a chore to get back to the strong mid-to-late game [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"the first few minutes of each run feel slow and repetitive. It becomes a chore to get back to the exciting mid-to-late game"}.

### Build variety & synergies
- Horizontal variety: with only ~30 items but a cap of 6 weapons + 6 passives, each run specializes into different strengths and weaknesses so it does not feel repetitive [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"you can somewhat specialize your character with different strengths & weaknesses each time, so it doesn't feel too repetitive"}.
- Characters and maps are the other variety axis: each character has unique starting weapons/passive bonuses, each map a distinct layout or hazard set, giving different starting conditions on the same loop [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"different starting conditions on the same core loop give players permission to experiment"}.
- Depth comes from combo-crafting ("what if I combine this weapon with that passive," "can I build to just stand still") rather than from per-upgrade choices, which are mostly auto-pilot [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"You start thinking, 'What if I combine this weapon with that passive?' or 'Can I create a build that lets me just stand still?'"}.

### Power fantasy & feel
- The whole run is a 0-to-100 ramp from a vulnerable single-attack character to an unstoppable screen-clearer, every session [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"Each session starts you as a vulnerable character with a single attack ... you transform into an unstoppable force"}.
- The XP magnet (vacuum item) is a designed dopamine spike: thousands of gems streak in and trigger rapid-fire level-ups, converting accumulated effort into a visible flood [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"Watching potentially thousands of experience gems streak towards you from off-screen, triggering rapid-fire level-ups is a huge dopamine hit"}.
- Reward cadence is kept tight: enemies drop XP, level-ups hand you a choice, chests pop with upgrades, so the gap between rewards almost never feels empty [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"barely lets you go a few seconds without something good happening ... That tight cadence tends to keep short play sessions from feeling empty"}.
- Readability aid: because the XP gems must be walked over to collect, movement and routing stay meaningful even as the screen fills [How Vampire Survivors built its own unlikely genre](https://store.epicgames.com/news/vampire-survivors-built-genre-autoshooter-bullet-heaven?lang=en-US){evidence:"You have to pick up the XP crystals that your enemies drop on defeat, which means that you have to think about your route through a level"}.

### Scaling math where documented
- **Additive stat caps**: Might, Area, Growth, and Greed pools are hard-capped at +1000% additive; items or Golden Eggs collected past that threshold add zero combat scaling [Vampire Survivors Weapon Evolution Engine Mechanics](https://twinfinite.net/guides/vampire-survivors-evolution-chart/){evidence:"additive pools for Might, Area, Growth, and Greed enforce a hard cap of +1000%. Any passive items or Golden Eggs collected after hitting the +1000% threshold yield zero functional scaling"}.
- **Multiplicative bypass**: Arcana effects are applied as an external multiplicative scalar that bypasses the +1000% additive ceiling, which is how end-game builds scale hitboxes and damage past normal limits [Vampire Survivors Weapon Evolution Engine Mechanics](https://twinfinite.net/guides/vampire-survivors-evolution-chart/){evidence:"M(arcana) is processed as an external scalar, it completely bypasses the +1000% ceiling, allowing players to scale weapon hitboxes and damage parameters beyond standard system limits"}.
- **PowerUp bonuses (representative, per the live list)**: Might +5%/rank to +25% (5 ranks), Cooldown -2.5%/rank to -5% (2 ranks), Amount +1 projectile (1 rank, 5000 gold), Growth +3%/rank to +15% XP, Greed +10%/rank to +50% gold, Curse +10%/rank to +50% (raises enemy speed/health/quantity/frequency) [PowerUps](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/PowerUps){evidence:"Might Raises inflicted damage by 5% per rank (max +25%) ... Cooldown Uses weapons 2.5% faster per rank (max 5%) ... Curse Increase enemy speed, health, quantity, and frequency by 10% per rank (max +50%)"}.
- **Optional run modifiers** stack intentionally for difficulty/reward tuning: Hyper, Hurry, Inverse, Endless, plus toggles for Arcanas and Limit Break [Inverse mode](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Inverse_mode){evidence:"All stages have four unlockable, toggleable modes: Hyper, Hurry, Inverse, and Endless mode ... whether to enable Arcanas and Limit Break"}. Limit Break lets a maxed build keep leveling weapons past their caps instead of being offered gold/chicken, which is the primary late-game scaling valve [Limit Break](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Limit_Break){evidence:"a fully upgraded build ... will present options to level their weapons beyond their usual limits"}.

## Brotato (Blobfish, 2023)

A wave-and-shop survivor-like: fixed-length runs broken into discrete waves with a shop between each. Combat is largely automatic but the player repositions and the between-wave shop is the strategic core.

### Core loop & run structure
- A standard run is **20 waves**; the goal is to survive all 20 and beat the final boss, buying and upgrading weapons and items between waves [Brotato Achievement Guide & Road Map](https://www.xboxachievements.com/game/brotato/guide/){evidence:"The goal is to survive all 20 waves and beat the boss at the end ... buy and upgrade weapons, buy new items, level up"}.
- Each wave is a timed survival burst; when it ends you auto-enter the shop (the first shop appears after wave 1, and the shop title shows the wave that just ended) [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"You enter the shop automatically after each wave (so the first shop is the one after the first wave). The shop title shows the number of the wave that just ended"}.
- **Difficulty is a selectable Danger ladder (0-5) plus Nightmare**, each inheriting the prior tier's modifiers, which is the main macro-progression axis per character [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"Each level inherits the difficulty increases from the levels before it"}.
- Meta-progression is unlock-based: clearing Danger tiers and runs unlocks new characters (the roster is the main persistent reward); there is no between-run stat-buy economy like Vampire Survivors' PowerUps [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"Danger 0 ... Unlocks One Armed ... Danger 5 ... Unlocks Demon"}. Endless Mode is a post-20 toggle for extended runs [Endless Mode](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Endless_Mode){evidence:"Endless Mode is a game mode that can be toggled on and off in the Menu when you are selecting a Danger Level to play"}.

### Weapon/ability acquisition & evolution
- Weapons come **only from the shop**; items can also drop from loot crates. You hold up to 6 weapons [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"It is the only source of weapons in the game (whereas items will also drop in the form of loot crates)"}.
- The "evolution" analogue is **combining**: two identical weapons of the same type and tier merge into one weapon of the next tier, up to a cap of Tier 4. Buying a duplicate when weapon slots are full auto-combines [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"This combines the weapon with another weapon of same type and tier into one weapon of one higher tier ... weapons cannot be combined above Tier 4"}.
- Level-ups (from accumulated XP) offer stat **Upgrades** rather than weapons; this upgrade screen can itself be rerolled using the same reroll economy as the shop [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"Rerolling is also possible when the character levels up and is offered a selection of upgrades. This rerolling uses the same prices as the Shop"}.
- Early-shop guarantees scaffold a build: waves 1-2 offer exactly 2 weapons + 2 items, waves 3-5 guarantee at least 1 weapon, and waves 1-5 add a bonus chance to re-offer your existing weapon class (higher the earlier the wave), so you can commit to a weapon type early [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"Wave 1 & 2: Exactly 2 Weapons + Exactly 2 Items. Wave 3, 4, & 5: Guaranteed 1 Weapon ... This bonus allows for a build selection in early game"}.

### Economy & shop
- **One resource, two faces**: Materials dropped by enemies simultaneously grant Experience (to level up) and Gold (to reroll and buy); the game uses the terms interchangeably [Materials](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Materials){evidence:"When you gain materials, you gain both Experience, used to level up, and Gold, the currency used to reroll and buy items in the Shop"}. Harvesting (a stat) grants a lump of materials at wave end [Stats](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Stats){evidence:"You earn x materials and XP at the end of a wave. Increased by 5% every time it activates"}.
- **Reroll economy** (the signature mechanic). The reroll increase and first-reroll price are wave-scaled, and each reroll adds a flat increase that resets each shop:
  - `Reroll Increase = Rounddown(0.40 * Wave) (min 1)`
  - `First Reroll Price = Rounddown(Wave * 0.75) + Reroll Increase`
  - Worked example: end of wave 5 gives increase = 2 and base = 3, so the first reroll costs 5 and each subsequent reroll adds +2 [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"the reroll increase is 0.40 * 5 = 2. and the base is 0.75 * 5 = 3.75, rounded down to 3. So the reroll price is 3+2 = 5 for the first reroll, and +2 for each subsequent roll"}.
  - There is no reroll cap other than affordability, and buying all 4 shop slots grants a free reroll [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"There is no limit to how many times you can reroll ... Buying all 4 items in the Shop also gives a free roll"}.
- **Item/weapon inflation**: `Final Price = (Base_Price + Wave + (Base_Price * 0.1 * Wave)) * Shop_Price`. The flat `+ Wave` term means cheap items inflate harder in relative terms and get less cost-effective late [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"cheaper items will be hit harder by inflation, and will be slightly less cost effective late game"}.
- **Locking** freezes an item's price and keeps it across rerolls and into the next wave, letting players save for a target [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"While an item is locked, it will stay in the shop when the shop is rerolled ... its price also remains locked and isn't increased by inflation"}.
- **Rarity gating by wave**: Tier 2 items appear from wave 2+, Tier 3 from wave 4+, Tier 4 from wave 8+; the per-wave rarity chance climbs toward documented max caps (e.g. Tier 4 maxes at 8% chance) and Luck multiplies these chances up to those caps [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"Tier 2 items can appear from Wave/Shop 2+, Tier 3 from Wave/Shop 4+, Tier 4 from Wave/Shop 8+ ... This value cannot exceed Max Chance"}.
- **Recycling** returns 25% of an item's current value by default, boostable to 60% (Recycling Machine) and 85% (plus Entrepreneur), scaling with inflated price [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"The default is to get 25% back when recycling. Recycling Machine can increase this value by 35% for a total of 60% back. This stacks with the +25% from Entrepreneur for a total of 85%"}.
- Economy items form a dedicated subsystem: Spyglass reduces reroll price (max 90% reduction), Coupon reduces item price (-5% each, permanent, max 50% / up to 80% with Entrepreneur), and some characters pay in Max HP (Demon) [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"Coupon reduces shop prices by -5% ... maximum permanent cost reduction you can have is currently 50% ... Demon - Pays for items Using Max HP"}.

### Enemy design & wave pacing
- Enemies drop materials with a documented **drop-chance decay**: 100% through wave 4, then reduced by 1.5% x wave number starting wave 5, flooring at 50% around wave 34; Horde waves multiply drops by 0.65 (35% fewer) [Materials](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Materials){evidence:"Starting at Wave 5, the chance is decreased by 1.5% times the wave number ... until it hits the minimum of 50% Chance ... During Horde Waves enemies have a 0,65 multiplier to their drop rate"}.
- **No material is ever lost**: on-map materials over the 50-blob cap merge into existing blobs, and uncollected materials are bagged and returned next wave (so the player is never punished for not vacuuming everything, which keeps the screen clean) [Materials](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Materials){evidence:"There is a limit of 50 materials that can be on the map at once ... any remaining materials on the map is collected and put into the Bag ... no materials are lost"}.
- **Danger-ladder scaling (concrete)**: Danger 1 adds new enemy types; Danger 2 introduces elites/hordes for 1 round (round 11 or 12); Danger 3 gives enemies +12% damage and +12% HP; Danger 4 spreads elites/hordes across rounds 11-18 and raises enemies to +26%/+26%; Danger 5 makes enemies +40%/+40% and spawns two bosses at once on round 20 (each at -25% HP). These are totals, not additive across tiers [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"Danger 5 ... Enemies deal 40% more damage and have 40% more HP ... Two Bosses spawn instead of one (round 20) ... The Health and Damage increases are the total for that danger level"}.
- Nightmare adds +60%/+60% HP/damage, +10% enemy speed, environmental projectiles and obscuring fog on top of Danger 5 [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"Enemy damage: +60% Enemy health: +60% Enemy speed: +10% Everything from Danger 5 Environmental projectiles appear Obscuring fog appears"}.
- Elites from Danger 2 drop a Legendary Crate (random Tier 4 item) and heal 100 HP on pickup; elites spawned on wave 12 or earlier have 75% HP to soften the first encounter [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"The Elites that appear ... always drop a Legendary Crate containing a random Tier 4 item and the crate heals 100 HP upon pickup ... Elites have 75% health when spawning on Wave 12 or earlier"}.
- **Accessibility sliders** separately scale enemy Damage/HP (25%-200%) and Speed (25%-150%), multiplicative with Danger, with the composite shown as a geometric mean, a clean tunable-difficulty model to copy [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"sliders ... change the Damage, Hit Points, and Speed of the enemies ... The modified difficulty is calculated as the geometric mean of the three sliders"}.

### Build variety & synergies
- Builds are defined primarily by **primary stats** stacked via items, most of which raise one stat while lowering another, forcing trade-off builds: Max HP, HP Regeneration, Life Steal, Damage, Melee/Ranged/Elemental Damage, Attack Speed, Crit Chance, Engineering, Range, Armor, Dodge, Speed, Luck, Harvesting [Stats](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Stats){evidence:"Most Items in the Shop increase one or more of your primary stats at the cost of often decreasing another one ... Primary Stats are the core way to scale into the late game"}.
- Weapons scale off whichever damage type they key to (Melee / Ranged / Elemental), so an item that boosts one type channels the whole build toward matching weapons, which is the core synergy driver [Stats](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Stats){evidence:"Modifies the base attack damage of weapons, if their damage scales with Melee Damage"}.
- **Documented soft caps** keep stats from running away: Attack Speed diminishes and cannot exceed 12 attacks/second, Crit Chance caps at 100%, Dodge caps at 60% (70-90% on specific characters), Life Steal can trigger only once per 0.1s (max 10 HP/s) [Stats](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Stats){evidence:"can't attack faster than 12 times per second ... Maximum 100% chance to do critical hits ... Dodge ... 60% (70% with Cryptid, 90% with Ghost) ... Can only trigger once per 0.1 second for a maximum of 10HP/second"}.
- Engineering is a parallel scaling lane (structures/turrets) that ignores most primary stats but benefits from secondary stats like piercing/explosion, enabling distinct turret builds [Stats](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Stats){evidence:"Increases the power of your structures. The other stats don't affect structures but specials do (piercing, bouncing, explosion etc.)"}.
- Characters are the main differentiator: wanted item tags, shop modifiers, melee/ranged restrictions, and special rules (Arms Dealer -95% weapon prices but weapons destroyed in shop; Fisherman guaranteed Bait; Mutant higher shop prices) all reshape the same loop [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"Arms Dealer - -95% Weapon Prices, Weapons are destroyed when entering the shop ... Fisherman - Shop always sells a Bait"}.

### Power fantasy & feel
- The feel is a stop-start rhythm: tense auto-combat wave, then a calm shop beat where the player cashes materials into power and plans the next wave, so the dopamine comes from the shop as much as the fight [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"You enter the shop automatically after each wave"}. The reroll gamble ("donkey roll" for a target weapon) is the between-wave slot machine [How To Win Danger 5 With All Characters](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2905085063){evidence:"You want to donkey roll for the weapon(s) you want and typically only buy weapons on the first 3 waves"}.

### Scaling math where documented
- Reroll: `Reroll Increase = Rounddown(0.40*Wave)` (min 1); `First Reroll Price = Rounddown(Wave*0.75) + Reroll Increase` [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"Reroll Increase: Rounddown(0.40 * Wave Number) (Minimum of 1) ... First Reroll Price: Rounddown(Wave Number * 0.75) + Reroll Increase"}.
- Price: `Final Price = (Base_Price + Wave + (Base_Price*0.1*Wave)) * Shop_Price`, rounded down, min 1 [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"Final Price = (Base_Price + Wave + (Base_Price * 0.1 * Wave)) * Shop_Price"}.
- Material drop decay: 100% to wave 4, then -1.5%*wave from wave 5, floor 50% [Materials](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Materials){evidence:"the chance is decreased by 1.5% times the wave number ... until it hits the minimum of 50% Chance"}.
- Danger HP/damage multipliers (totals): D3 +12%, D4 +26%, D5 +40%, Nightmare +60% [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"Enemies deal 40% more damage and have 40% more HP"}.
- Armor conversion: each point of armor means it takes ~6.66% more damage to kill you [Stats](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Stats){evidence:"Each point of armor means it takes 6.66% more damage to kill you"}.

## Halls of Torment (Chasing Carrots, 2023)

A diablo-flavored survivor-like on Godot, with equipment loadouts, a deeper stat sheet, and a secondary "Abilities" layer on top of weapon traits [Halls of Torment Wiki](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Halls_of_Torment_Wiki){evidence:"Halls of Torment is a game originally developed by Chasing Carrots on Godot Engine for Steam"}.

### Core loop & run structure
- Each run drops you into one Hall; you survive the horde for **30 minutes** and then face that Hall's final boss (a Lord). Mini-bosses appear at set times with telegraphed moves [Halls of Torment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halls_of_Torment){evidence:"At various set times throughout each stage, mini bosses will appear ... If the player survives the 30-minute run, they will face one of the Lords"}.
- Five primary stages escalate in difficulty, each with its own hazards, a strict timed hierarchy of elites, and a multi-phase Lord [Halls of Torment Stages & Bosses](https://shapes.inc/fandom/halls-of-torment/stages-and-bosses){evidence:"five primary stages, each presenting unique environmental hazards and a strictly timed hierarchy of elite monsters ... a final boss with complex, multi-phase attack patterns"}.
- **Meta-progression has three persistent layers**: Gold-bought **Blessings** (the permanent version of Traits, applied to all heroes, fully refundable at the Shrine of Blessings after the 300-gold Obolus milestone), **Equipment** retrieved via the Well (see below), and quest/milestone unlocks (characters, traits, XP-gain boosts) [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"Blessings are essentially the permanent version of Traits ... can be refunded from the Shrine of Blessings ... will not become available until you've collected a total of 300 Gold to complete the Obolus Milestone"}.
- **The Well** is the signature meta mechanic: items looted in a run are not kept unless you carry them to the Well and "retrieve" them, after which they can be purchased permanently from the Wellkeeper and equipped from the Item Stash in camp [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"The Well is a meta-progression mechanic which allows looted items to be reused in future rounds ... Move to and interact with the Well ... In the Camp, select the Wellkeeper and purchase the item"}. Sending an item up the Well depletes it for that round, so the number of "Buckets" limits how many items you can bank per run [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"Sending an Equipment up the Well depletes it for that round. Collecting Extra Buckets increases its number of uses"}.
- Runs are explicitly goal-driven early on: because the hero is under-leveled, the design nudges players to enter with a specific Quest or Achievement target rather than a blind clear, and quest completion boosts XP gain to speed later runs [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"Because you're under-leveled from the outset ... try choosing Quests or Achievements to complete ... Completing Quests also increases your XP Gain"}.

### Weapon/ability acquisition & evolution
- Each hero has a unique **Main Weapon**. Leveling up (from XP gems) offers one of four **Traits** that either augment that Main Weapon or boost base Attributes [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"you'll Level Up and get rewarded with your choice of one of four Traits that can either improve your Hero's unique Main Weapon or boost their base Attributes"}.
- **Abilities** are a separate acquisition track: you pick one of three Ability options from a **Scroll of Mastery** (floor loot or elite drops), adding extra automated attacks alongside the Main Weapon [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"Abilities are collected by acquiring a Scroll of Mastery and selecting one of three Ability options. Scrolls of Mastery can be found as floor loot or by defeating some elite enemies"}.
- **Trait tiers are gated by Hero Level**: to unlock the next tier of a Trait you must reach a specific Player Level, so deeper upgrades are time/XP-gated within the run [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"Trait tiers are locked behind Player Level, so to unlock the next tier of a Trait, you'll need to reach a specific Player Level"}.
- Temporary **Power-Ups** (from crates/barrels) are expiring timed buffs, distinct from permanent-in-run Traits/Abilities, adding a pickup-chase layer [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"Power-Ups are temporary upgrades that will leave your inventory when their brief timer expires"}.
- There is no Vampire-Survivors-style weapon-plus-passive fusion; instead "evolution" of power comes from stacking Trait tiers, layering Abilities, and (via Agony) rolling higher Equipment rarities with Growth variants that scale with levels gained while equipped [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"Certain items have a Growth (Uncommon) or Growth ↑ (Rare) variant, with a bonus that scales with levels gained while the item is equipped"}.

### Economy & shop
- No in-run shop; the "shop" is the camp (Wellkeeper for Equipment, Shrine of Blessings for stat buys), funded by Gold that is kept even if you abandon a run [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"Even if you abandon a run, you'll still pocket the Gold acquired. Gold Gain can be improved by purchasing Blessings"}.
- Equipment loadout structure (worth copying for slot-based builds): one each of Helmet/Amulet/Chest/Boots/Gloves plus two Rings (seven worn pieces), with up to 4 saved Loadouts, plus a 4-slot in-run Bag for extra items [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"Only 1 equipment of each type can be equipped, except for Rings of which 2 can be equipped ... You can save up to 4 Loadouts"}.
- **Agony / Torment** is the end-game difficulty+reward dial: with Agony enabled, chests can roll Uncommon/Rare items, and the min/max rarity of chest rolls is a function of the summed Torment Rank + Agony Rank (e.g. Standard chests floor at Common up to TR+AR 11, allow Rare at TR+AR >= 16) [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"In Agony, Items rolled in Chests have their minimum and maximum rarities determined by the sum of Torment Rank (TR) & Agony Rank (AR)"}. Item roll weights are rarity-based: Common 4, Uncommon 2, Rare 1 [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"Common: 4 ... Uncommon: 2 ... Rare: 1"}.

### Enemy design & wave pacing
- Immediate swarm on entry; difficulty is a 30-minute ramp where both the horde and the player grow in power, so under-leveling is intended early [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"you'll immediately get swarmed by monsters ... The more enemies you defeat and the longer you endure ... the same can be said for your enemies"}.
- A displayed "Time Remaining" counts down to the Lord; as it shrinks, enemies rise in strength and number, giving the player a clear pressure gauge [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"The amount of time left until the Hall's final boss spawns ... As the remaining time dwindles, you'll enemies will increase in strength and number"}.
- Enemy readability is a design axis: ranged/projectile enemies are the priority targets, and the game deliberately mixes weak and passive enemy types that stay farmable for XP and safe repositioning even late in a dense run [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"keep an eye out for weaker enemies ... can be farmed to quickly collect XP ... some monsters are more passive than their charging allies"}.
- Technical honesty for a Pokemon-themed clone: performance is single-core CPU bound and per-frame spawn rate is capped for performance, so higher frame rates actually raise spawn density, a concrete reason to budget enemy counts carefully [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"Spawn rate in each frame is limited for performance reasons. Having a higher frame rate can increase spawn rate"}.
- Common critique to design against: some players feel the important decisions all land in the first ~5 minutes and the rest becomes walking the map, a mid-run flatness risk [Halls of Torment General Discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/discussions/0/7008219766205837003/){evidence:"all the important stuff happens in the first 5 or so minutes, then it just becomes a walk around the map in the hope something happens"}.

### Build variety & synergies
- Deeper stat sheet than its peers, with tagged bonuses: each stat bonus is Base (flat, added) or Multiplier (%, multiplied), and the final value is base-sum then multiplier-sum applied by matching tags (e.g. Main Weapon, Summon, Physical, elemental) [Stats](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Stats){evidence:"Base Bonuses: These get added to the base stat ... Multiplier Bonuses: These get multiplied ... obtain all bonuses with that source's tags and all untagged bonuses"}.
- **Over-100% stacking** is a distinctive scaling model: several stats are allowed to exceed 100% without clamping, and the excess becomes additional stacks (projectiles/attacks for Multistrike, extra crit/effect rolls for Crit Chance "over-crit" and Effect Chance) via deterministic or biased-coin methods [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"certain stats in Halls of Torment are allowed to exceed 100% without clamping. When these values exceed 100%, multiple stacks can be applied"}.
- Crit is multiplicative and stacking: on crit, Final Damage = Normal x (1 + Crit Bonus), and over-crit adds whole extra crit stacks (e.g. 310% crit = 90% chance of 3 stacks, 10% of 4) [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"Crit Chance: 310% (90% chance for 3 stacks, 10% chance for 4 stacks) ... Final Crit Damage: 90% chance for 380 ... 10% chance for 490"}.
- Defensive builds use two independent layers: **Block** (chance to fully negate a hit, driven by Block Strength, no partial reduction) checked before **Defense** (ratio-based damage reduction, with Effective HP rising ~4.17% per Defense point above 100) [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"Block ... determines the chance to negate an incoming attack entirely ... At 100 Defense and above, Effective HP increases linearly at a rate of 4.17% per point"}.
- Equipment Growth variants reward long-run commitment: a Growth bonus equals the Boost bonus at Level 100, Growth-up equals Boost at Level 50, enabling "scales with my level" builds, and certain items follow an explicit power-law input/output relation (diminishing returns when the exponent is below 1) [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"Growth bonus = Boost bonus at Level 100 ... Certain items provide an Output that is related to some Input by a power law"}.

### Power fantasy & feel
- The ramp is the same prey-to-predator arc, but gated harder up front: weapons and abilities cannot outpace the horde until the build is stacked, so constant repositioning and farming weak enemies is the moment-to-moment skill [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"your Weapons, and Abilities will never be able to outpace the horde ... constantly reposition to dodge incoming blows but don't stray outside of your effective attack radius"}.
- Readability is actively taught: piercing + area let you hit clusters, and the recommended technique is circling a mob while aiming at its center, which both maximizes piercing and keeps the hero out of the mass [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"try circling a large group of creatures while continually aiming toward the center of the cluster"}.
- The Well + Blessings + quest unlocks give the between-run "I keep something" payoff that prevents dead runs, same role as Vampire Survivors' PowerUps but spread across equipment, stats, and roster [Halls of Torment Guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/halls-of-torment/Beginner's_Guide){evidence:"Blessings and Equipment are upgrades that can carry over from run to run"}.

### Scaling math where documented
- Final stat value = (base + sum of Base bonuses) x (1 + sum of Multiplier bonuses) by tag; example 2/s base attack speed + 0.3 base + 100% = 4.6/s [Stats](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Stats){evidence:"Base Attack Speed: 2/s ... Attack Speed Base Bonus: +0.3/s ... Multiplier Bonus: +100% ... Final Attack Speed = 4.6/s"}.
- Defense example: 40 Defense = 46% damage reduction (50 incoming becomes 27 taken); reduction cannot drop damage below 1 [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"Defense: 40 ... Damage Reduction: 46% ... Damage Taken: 27"}.
- Block example: Block Strength 40 vs 50 incoming = 40% block chance; 100% block requires block strength = damage x 4 [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"Block Strength: 40 ... Incoming Damage: 50 Block Chance: 40%"}.
- Agony chest rarity thresholds by TR+AR (Standard chests): Common up to 11, Uncommon up to 23, Rare at 16+; Champion chests allow Rare at 11+ [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"Standard & Bottle Chests ... Common ≤ 11 ... Uncommon ≤ 23 ... Rare ≥ 16 ... Champion Chests ... Rare ≥ 11"}.
- Damage Mod/Reduction per hit (pierce/duration falloff) is raised by Force but can never reach x1.00, a built-in cap on falloff-mitigation builds [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"which is increased by Force but cannot reach ×1.00"}.

## Cross-game design principles for survivor-likes

What the genre has converged on, the recurring pitfalls, and what keeps the loop addictive. Each principle is written as actionable guidance for the Pokemon-themed build.

### 1. Collapse inputs to one verb and automate the rest
The genre's foundational move is to reduce the player to movement/positioning and auto-fire everything else, which forces enemy waves and routing to carry the design weight and makes each step matter [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"builds everything around a single verb: move ... forces positioning and enemy waves to carry all the weight"}. The core challenge then reads from a single screenshot (one health bar amid hundreds of enemies), which is why onboarding is near-instant [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"the core challenge is crystal clear, and you can understand the stakes from a single screenshot"}.

### 2. Nest three loops: second-to-second, per-run, cross-run
Every converged example runs a micro loop (dodge/collect), a meso loop (survive the run and build up), and a macro loop (persistent unlocks), and pulling any layer out collapses the game [Understanding a Gameplay Loop](https://gamedesigning.org/learn/game-loop/){evidence:"Pull any layer out and the game collapses"}. All three games cap the run at a fixed length (20 waves in Brotato, ~30 minutes in Vampire Survivors and Halls of Torment) with a terminal boss as the goalpost [Brotato Achievement Guide & Road Map](https://www.xboxachievements.com/game/brotato/guide/){evidence:"survive all 20 waves and beat the boss at the end"}. Note the practical ceiling: community sentiment holds that an uninterrupted single run should probably sit around 15-20 minutes max for this type of game [Be My Horde General Discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2499520/discussions/0/6982351509010766772/){evidence:"15 minutes, MAYBE 20 minutes is the maximum a reasonable single sitting in this type of game should take"}.

### 3. Never let a run feel wasted (meta-progression as anti-frustration)
The persistent layer exists as much to kill "zero-progress" runs as to add power: Vampire Survivors banks gold into all-character PowerUps, Halls of Torment keeps gold and banks equipment through the Well, Brotato unlocks the next character/Danger tier [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"eliminating 'zero progress' runs: even if you didn't make it as far ... you want to retry immediately with your new perks unlocked"}. Keep the increments tiny and split across many small bonuses so it does not feel grindy even as it secretly multiplies the baseline (Vampire Survivors hides a ~2.5x damage / ~2x health swing this way) [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"The increments are tiny individually and again separated into many little bonuses, so it doesn't feel grindy"}.

### 4. Smooth the power curve with many small rewards, not a few big ones
Trickle progression (many upgrade tiers across few items) beats a step curve: it keeps a reward landing roughly every 20-25 seconds and keeps the game frequently paused for a pick, which also relieves visual strain [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"A trickling stat progression helps to maintain the pacing better than a step-like difficulty curve ... the game is more frequently paused while you're picking an upgrade"}. The explicit target: minimize the gap between rewards so a short session never feels empty [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"How long is the average gap between rewards in your game ... If those moments are spaced too far apart"}. Occasional big bumps are fine and feel rewarding as long as difficulty catches back up [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"smoothing out the progression curve doesn't exclude the possibility of major bumbs ... The game's difficulty inevitably catch up afterwards"}.

### 5. Combine controlled choice with a slot-machine windfall
Each game pairs deterministic level-up picks with a randomized windfall: Vampire Survivors' chests (hardcoded 1-1-3-1-5 opening to seed high expectations, then real odds) and the XP magnet; Brotato's reroll gambling; Halls of Torment's elite chest / Scroll of Mastery rolls [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"Mixing controlled choices with occasional random windfalls keeps decisions feeling strategic while still delivering surprise"}. The designer explicitly borrowed online-casino techniques for the chest animation [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"his work on online casino games had inspired him. The chest opening animation ... feels like a slot machine"}.

### 6. Force specialization with hard inventory/slot caps
Caps (Vampire Survivors 6+6, Brotato 6 weapons, Halls of Torment 7 equipment slots + abilities) are what make a "build" distinct run to run and create the trade-off decisions; Brotato sharpens this further by having most items raise one stat while lowering another [Stats](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Stats){evidence:"Most Items in the Shop increase one or more of your primary stats at the cost of often decreasing another one"}. Combo-crafting (weapon + passive, or stat-type channeling) is the real depth, not the individual upgrade pick [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"Discovering powerful synergies and experimenting with different character strengths provides compelling long-term goals"}.

### 7. Gate the big power spikes behind two conditions, not one
Vampire Survivors' evolution is the template: it requires both a maxed base weapon + catalyst passive AND a time-gated chest, so the payoff is earned and legible, not accidental [Evolution](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Evolution){evidence:"a fully leveled base weapon and its counterpart ... must then collect a Treasure Chest that contains an evolution type reward"}. Brotato's tier-combine (two identical weapons merge up a tier, capped at Tier 4) and Halls of Torment's level-gated Trait tiers are the same idea in different clothes [Shop](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop){evidence:"This combines the weapon with another weapon of same type and tier into one weapon of one higher tier"}.

### 8. Make density legible and performant
High-density readability is a first-class problem. Techniques observed: requiring XP pickup so routing stays meaningful [How Vampire Survivors built its own unlikely genre](https://store.epicgames.com/news/vampire-survivors-built-genre-autoshooter-bullet-heaven?lang=en-US){evidence:"you have to think about your route through a level"}; never losing uncollected currency so the player is not forced to vacuum a cluttered screen (Brotato bags leftovers and caps on-map blobs at 50) [Materials](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Materials){evidence:"no materials are lost, and you just collect them on the next wave"}; and keeping all art/UI/SFX at one consistent fidelity so a packed screen still reads as intentional [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"Aim for Cohesion Before You Aim for Polish ... Nothing in the game looks or sounds out of place"}. Budget spawn counts against the engine: these games are typically single-core CPU bound and spawn rate is frame-capped [Game Mechanics](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics){evidence:"Game performance depends heavily on single-core CPU performance ... Spawn rate in each frame is limited for performance reasons"}.

### 9. Scale enemies on simple, multiplicative, tunable dials
All three scale difficulty with blunt global multipliers rather than per-enemy rebalancing: Brotato's Danger ladder applies total HP/damage multipliers (+12/+26/+40/+60%) plus elite/boss-count modifiers and inherits lower tiers, with independent accessibility sliders multiplied on top [Danger Levels](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Dangers){evidence:"The Health and Damage increases are the total for that danger level ... These increases are multiplicative with the Accessibility Sliders"}. Vampire Survivors' Curse and Halls of Torment's Agony/Torment are the same lever: one knob that raises enemy stats/quantity and, in Halls, simultaneously raises loot rarity so harder = more rewarding [Item](https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Item){evidence:"When Agony is enabled, Uncommon or Rare items have a chance to drop in chests"}.

### 10. Cap runaway stats, then provide a multiplicative bypass for the fantasy
A recurring two-tier math pattern: additive pools are capped (Vampire Survivors +1000%; Brotato soft caps like 12 attacks/sec, 100% crit, 60% dodge; Halls Damage Mod cannot reach x1.00), while a separate multiplicative layer (Vampire Survivors Arcanas, Halls over-100% stacking and crit multipliers) exists to deliver the "I broke the game" late ceiling [Vampire Survivors Weapon Evolution Engine Mechanics](https://twinfinite.net/guides/vampire-survivors-evolution-chart/){evidence:"M(arcana) is processed as an external scalar, it completely bypasses the +1000% ceiling"}. This gives you balance control in the common case and spectacle at the top end.

### Common pitfalls to avoid
- **Flat / front-loaded power curve.** If all meaningful decisions land in the first few minutes, the mid-run goes slack and players disengage; this is Halls of Torment's most-cited complaint [Halls of Torment General Discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/discussions/0/7008219766205837003/){evidence:"all the important stuff happens in the first 5 or so minutes, then it just becomes a walk around the map"}.
- **The early-game tax and late-game grind.** Once mastered, the slow opening minutes become a chore and the escalating meta-cost can push players into gold-optimization over experimentation; keep the best part from being sandwiched between a dull ramp-up and a distant finish line [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"The early-game tax and late-game grind bookend the best part of the experience"}.
- **Bosses that test the build, not the player.** DPS-check bosses after a long run feel like failing an audit; mix in dodgeable, telegraphed attack patterns (Halls of Torment's multi-phase Lords) so the terminal fight rewards skill too [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"When a boss tests your build instead of your skill, victory feels more like passing an audit"}.
- **Choice paralysis / meaningless choices (two failure modes).** Vampire Survivors errs toward meaningless (declined options reappear, upgrades are predetermined), which keeps flow but thins strategy [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"choices aren't significant for the most part (since the options you decline will probably show up again"}. The counter-tools the genre uses to keep choices meaningful without overwhelming are Reroll, Skip, and Banish (Vampire Survivors PowerUps) and reroll/lock (Brotato), letting players sculpt the offered pool instead of accepting paralysis [PowerUps](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/PowerUps){evidence:"Reroll ... Skip ... Banish ... allows you to remove an item from level up choices, for the rest of the run"}.
- **Unreadable screens / lost pickups** are mitigated by the density techniques in Principle 8.

### What makes the loop addictive (summary)
The compounding of visible, audible escalation ("glance at the screen and see how much stronger you are than five minutes ago") plus a tight reward cadence plus a persistent layer that forbids wasted runs plus achievement/quest side-goals that justify replaying the same content [How Power Fantasy Creates Addictive Gameplay](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/power-fantasy/vampire-survivors){evidence:"Power fantasy tends to sell itself when players can glance at the screen and see how much stronger they are than they were five minutes ago"}; [The Secret Sauce of Vampire Survivors](https://jboger.substack.com/p/the-secret-sauce-of-vampire-survivors){evidence:"its immense achievements list, giving you constant side objectives while playing the same content repeatedly ... it always goes the extra mile to put a cherry on the top"}. The genre's own retrospective framing: minimalist gameplay made endlessly engaging by this layering, which is why dozens of developers have iterated on the formula since 2022 [Roguelike? That's Old News. Survivors-like is the New Wave](https://lordsofgaming.net/2025/12/roguelike-thats-old-news-survivors-like-is-the-new-wave/){evidence:"a formula that makes minimalist gameplay feel endlessly engaging"}.

### Pokemon-theme mapping cheatsheet (derived, not sourced)
These are design suggestions inferred from the patterns above, not claims from any source:
- Map "weapon + passive evolution" onto Pokemon evolution lines (base mon fully leveled + an evolution-stone "catalyst" item + a time/boss-gated trigger = evolved mon with inherited + new effects), mirroring Vampire Survivors' two-condition gate.
- Use typed damage (fire/water/electric) as Brotato-style stat channels so items that boost a type pull the whole team toward matching moves, creating synergy pressure.
- Treat caught/benched Pokemon like Halls of Torment's Well/Loadout: a persistent collection you bank between runs, so no run is wasted.
- Keep one blunt difficulty knob (a Danger/Agony analogue) that raises wild-enemy HP/damage/density and loot rarity together.

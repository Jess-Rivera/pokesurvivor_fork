Lost Pokemon Talk: Research and Build Path (deferred feature, 2026-11-21, DRAFT)

Feature: when you deliver a Lost Pokemon to the mark, a short Persona or SMT style exchange nudges whether it joins. It is deferred until #138 (S7) and #146 land. This file is research plus a build path, with no code. Every number tagged [Proposed] is a starting value to playtest.

1. The constraint that shapes everything

The repo's narrative rules forbid the obvious version, where the Pokemon asks you questions. Pokemon do not speak conversationally in this game [1]. Wild Pokemon also must not hint at the code, while still behaving naturally within their personalities [1].

So the adaptation flips the SMT format:

SMT / Persona This game
The demon asks, you answer The game narrates a moment ("Eevee hesitates at the mark."), and you pick an action
The demon replies in speech The Pokemon reacts without words: an emote bubble, its cry, a hop or a recoil
Personality is a hidden tone Personality is the nature, which the toast already names after S7

That makes it closer to Undertale's ACT menu than to a conversation. In Undertale each monster has its own actions, and you can spare it once its conditions are met [2].

2. How SMT does it

  • Loop. You answer the demon's questions and meet its demands, and a satisfied demon then joins [3]. In SMT V that means 1 or 2 sets of dialogue, sometimes branching into a longer chain [4].
  • Personality. It is hidden. Nocturne gives every demon invisible tone, gender and age properties [4]. The same scenario can turn out differently depending on personality [4].
  • Demands. After the talk, the demon asks for Macca, items, HP or MP until an invisible "enough" value is reached [4]. It can also take your gifts and leave without giving anything back [3].
  • Modifiers. Moon phase shifts moods, and some demons are cheerful at full moon while others are irritable [5]. A level gate means a weak protagonist is not worth allying with [3].
  • Numbers. No mainline SMT game documents how much one answer moves the mood. We have to author our own.

3. How Persona does it

Persona 5 (the simple template). The Shadow's personality shows on screen: Upbeat, Timid, Irritable or Gloomy. Each one maps to a preferred answer type: Funny, Serious, Vague or Kind [6]. Each personality has one liked type, one neutral type and two hated types [7]. You get two questions, and the answers carry no labels [6]. Community lists say a success needs 2 liked answers, or 1 liked plus 1 neutral [8]. A neutral second answer gets you an item instead of a recruit [4].

Persona 2 Contact (the meter template). There are four reactions: Pleasure, Interest, Anger and Fear [9]. Each one shows as a colored aura and resolves after three hits. For example, three Fear reactions make the demon run [9]. Players found it tedious [10].

Takeaway. P5's two questions and visible label are the right length for a pause inside a horde run. From P2, borrow only the wordless colored reaction.

4. What players hate, and what to borrow

Complaint Source Our rule
The personality is hidden and only readable from looks, so you end up on a wiki [11] Show the nature and its stat arrows on the talk window
Obtuse and unpredictable [12] Reactions follow one rule, derived from mainline nature data
Gifts don't guarantee a join [13] Talking costs nothing, and the delivery heal and coins always fire
One or two line demons with no character [14] Several narration lines per temperament, with species type flavor where cheap
Flat odds feel like a grind (DQV recruits by pure chance) [15] The talk moves the S7 star odds visibly

What players liked: P5's talk made combat a risk trade, since you skip the free finishing attack [12]. Our version of that risk is the escort itself, so delivery quality feeds the talk.

Genre fit. Survivor-likes already pause often for picks, and that pause also relieves visual strain [16]. So a paused two-prompt beat fits the genre. It must stay short so the reward cadence holds.

6. The design: nature drives everything

Mainline data already encodes a personality. A nature's liked flavor matches the stat it raises, and its disliked flavor matches the stat it lowers [18]. The game already stores each nature as a name plus raised and lowered stat positions, in js/data/species-traits.js NATURES (generated from pokeapi). All 25 rows check out against the repo's pokeapi-master natures.csv. So the reaction table is derived, never hand authored.

  N --> L["lowered stat"]
  R --> LA["its approach and its berry<br/>react LIKE"]
  L --> DA["its approach and its berry<br/>react DISLIKE"]
  N --> O["the other 3 stats<br/>react NEUTRAL"]
  N -- "neutral nature<br/>(Hardy, Docile, Serious, Bashful, Quirky)" --> NB["LIKE = the species' highest<br/>non-HP base stat; no DISLIKE"]

Labels and approach names are [Proposed] English text (CLAUDE.md i18n: new strings English only).

Two prompts (the P5 length). 1. Approach. Narration sets the scene, then you pick one of 3 approaches drawn from the 5. The liked one is always among them (D3). 2. Offer. You pick one of 3 berry flavors. The berries are flavor only and come from no inventory, so nothing is paid and refused.

Favor and odds [Proposed]. Like +1, Neutral 0, Dislike -1 per prompt. Delivering the Pokemon above half HP adds +1 (escort bonus), so favor runs from -2 to +3. The final chance is the S7 star base times a favor multiplier:

Favor x 1 star (base 20%) 2 star (base 12%) 3 star (base 6%)
-2 0.25 5 3 1.5
-1 0.5 10 6 3
0 1 20 12 6
+1 1.5 30 18 9
+2 2.25 45 27 13.5
+3 3 60 36 18

Favor 0 reproduces today's S7 odds exactly, and star order holds on every row. So S7's T2 relation survives unchanged.

Feedback. After each pick, an emote shows over the portrait: a note for Like, a sweat drop for Neutral, a scowl for Dislike. The P5 icon idea comes from the community lists [8]. The cry plays at a pitch that follows the reaction, and the portrait rim tints like a P2 aura. After each reaction the band moves, using the same band the catch window already shows [19].

Overlap to watch. catch.js already groups natures into flee buckets (easy, default, hard) as [Open] playtest values [19]. Those buckets don't follow raised stats. A Timid Pokemon is "hard" to catch there but would love Play chase here. That may be fine (skittish but friendly), but it is two nature models side by side. Flag it at the #148 tuning pass.

7. Flow and state machine

flowchart TD
  A["Lost Pokemon toast:<br/>A Timid Eevee is lost!"] --> B["Touch it: it follows you"]
  B --> C{"Reach the mark in 120 s<br/>and it survives?"}
  C -- no --> X["It leaves (unchanged)"]
  C -- yes --> D["Delivery: 50% heal and coins<br/>fire first (unchanged)"]
  D --> E["Run pauses, talk window opens"]
  E --> F["Prompt 1: approach"]
  F --> G["Reaction, band moves"]
  G --> H["Prompt 2: berry"]
  H --> I["Reaction, band moves"]
  I --> J{"One roll at the final chance"}
  J -- join --> K["Held recruit (G.Catch held),<br/>settled at run end"]
  J -- no --> M["It thanks you and leaves"]
  K --> Z["Run resumes"]
  M --> Z

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Opening: delivered
  Opening --> AskApproach
  AskApproach --> ReactApproach: pick
  ReactApproach --> AskOffer
  AskOffer --> ReactOffer: pick
  ReactOffer --> Resolve
  Resolve --> Joined: roll passes
  Resolve --> Left: roll fails
  AskApproach --> Resolve: Esc, skip at current favor
  AskOffer --> Resolve: Esc, skip at current favor
  Joined --> [*]
  Left --> [*]

Skipping (Esc) resolves at the current favor, never worse, so a player who doesn't want the beat loses nothing against S7. Everything lives only for the run, so there is no new save field and no migration.

End-goal test relations (TEST-FIRST: relations, not values).

# Relation
R1 For every non-neutral nature, the approach and berry of its raised stat react Like, those of its lowered stat react Dislike, and the other three react Neutral
R2 For neutral natures, Like is the species' highest non-HP base stat, and nothing reacts Dislike
R3 The chance never falls as favor rises and never goes above the cap
R4 At every favor value, 1 star beats 2 star, and 2 star beats 3 star
R5 Favor 0 matches S7's 20 / 12 / 6% within tolerance over seeded runs
R6 The liked approach and the liked berry are always among the 3 options offered (if D3 is a)
R7 Delivery heal and coins are identical whatever the answers, including Esc
R8 A join adds one held catch with the toast's nature, and the Pokebox only changes at settle (S7 T3 still passes)
R9 No prompt or reaction string is quoted speech from the Pokemon (META-NARRATIVE rule), and every string is English

9. Decisions needed before T1

# Question Options Pick
D1 When does the talk happen? a. at delivery / b. at first touch, before the escort a: it is already S7's roll moment, and the escort can feed favor
D2 Neutral natures a. all Neutral, escort bonus only / b. Like = highest base stat / c. a hidden random Like b: readable from the species, so no guessing
D3 Is the liked option always offered? a. yes / b. random 3 of 5 a: it makes the talk a read-the-nature puzzle, not luck
D4 Show the stat arrows? a. "Timid (+Spe -Atk)" / b. name only a: P5 shows its label, and SMT's hidden one sends players to wikis
D5 How the chance shows a. reuse the catch band / b. a pip mood meter a: one way to show odds across the game
D6 Does a bad talk ever cost the delivery rewards? a. never / b. two Dislikes forfeit the coins a: it is a rescue, and the "paid but refused" feeling is the top complaint

Deferred hooks. Co-op guests would only see the host's result. That is a deferred issue, not built. A Pokedex memory of reactions per species (like SMT if... loyalty) would be a new save field with a null default. It is optional and not in T1 to T4.

Gaps in the research. SMT IV and IV Apocalypse are barely documented. P5's exact scoring is community readings only. Pokemon Mystery Dungeon, Cassette Beasts and Disco Elysium were not covered.
